The rules, fixed in advance

  1. Every pick gets {{ amount|rupees }} of pretend money, bought as whole shares at the first market opening price after it was published. If one share costs more than that, one share is bought.
  2. Each trade is sold if the price falls to its {{ term("stop", "safety level") }}, or when its {{ term("window", "holding window") }} ends: 5 {{ term("marketday", "market days") }} for "mostly excitement", 10 for "some real news", 15 for "backed by business results".
  3. Every trade pays real {{ term("charges", "charges") }}, and profits pay {{ term("tax", "short-term gains tax") }}.
  4. A pick is not bought if it opens at or below its safety level.
  5. A share is a new pick once. It isn't picked again while its trade is running, and afterwards only if something new has been confirmed.
  6. Trades are never changed afterwards. If the rules change, earlier trades keep the rules they were made under.{% if p.trades|selectattr('t.rules_version', 'equalto', 1)|list %} Trades made under the first rules shared ₹40,000 a day between that day's picks.{% endif %}

"Why it moved" is chosen by fixed rules from what moved with the price: company news, the whole market, or the sector. That is strong evidence of the cause, not proof. The Nifty 50 and fixed-deposit comparisons put the same money in on the same days; the fixed deposit assumes 7% a year.
