How it works

Everything this app does, explained without jargon. Five minutes to read.

{{ icon("info") }}What this app is

A research assistant for the Indian stock market. Every evening it looks at about 750 companies listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE). Every morning at 8 am it emails you up to {{ per_group }} shares in each size group (large, mid-size and small companies) that have a real, checkable reason to rise over the next few days to three weeks.

It is not a prediction and not advice. Shares can always fall. It shows its homework so you can decide.

{{ icon("filter") }}How a share gets picked

  1. Safety first. Shares that are hard to sell, on the exchange's watch list, or whose owners have borrowed heavily against them are dropped.
  2. Something is happening. The price is rising, more people are trading it, or the company announced something.
  3. Homework. The app reads the company's official announcements, results, owner holdings and news from trusted newspapers.
  4. A score from 1 to 5, based on six things: business health, price trend, fresh news, pending orders, who owns it, and what the news says.
  5. Only strong cases are picked: {{ min_rating }} or higher, with a confirmed reason and every safety check passed. Some days nothing qualifies, and the email says so.
  6. No repeats. A share is a new pick once. While its pretend trade is running it is listed under "Still running", not picked again.

What the labels mean

Backed by business results

The company itself reported something solid, like a big profit jump, and the price hasn't run too far yet.

Hold: up to 3 weeks.

Some real news, some excitement

There's a real reason to like it, but also a catch: the news is only partly confirmed, the business is average, or the price already jumped.

Hold: up to 2 weeks, and sell if it falls to the safety level.

Mostly excitement

The price is rising mainly because people are excited, not because the business improved.

Hold: 3 to 5 trading days only. Decide when you'll sell before you buy.

{{ icon("shield") }}Where the information comes from

  • Official company announcements to the NSE. Companies are punished for false ones, so these count on their own.
  • Trusted newspapers (Economic Times, Mint, Business Standard, Moneycontrol and others). A story counts fully only when two of them report it.
  • Broker opinions ("buy, target Rs 500") are shown, but barely count: they're opinions, not facts.
  • Never used: social media, WhatsApp forwards, Telegram tips, YouTube, blogs.

Every claim in a report links to its source and to a saved copy, so you can read it yourself.

{{ icon("target") }}How the app is held to account

The App's track record pretends to buy every pick with {{ amount|rupees }}, using real prices, real costs and tax, and sells by fixed rules. It shows, in rupees, what happened to each pick and why: the business, the whole market, its sector, or no clear reason. It also follows the stocks the app said no to.

In My practice portfolio you can make your own pretend trades the same way.

Use real money only if the track record is good over at least 20 finished trades.
